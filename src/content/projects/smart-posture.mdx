<CaseSection num="01" title="Problem">

Detect bad sitting posture in real time and correct it straight away, with haptic feedback on a wearable.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection num="02" title="Architecture">

A camera and the host PC classify posture with a Python, OpenCV and dlib face-pose pipeline, fused with IMU data from the wearable. The result goes over UDP to a XIAO ESP32-C3 on a custom PCB, which drives a vibration motor. Blynk carries monitoring and alerts to a phone.

<CaseSection num="03" title="Key decisions">

<Decisions>
  <Decision title="Where the vision runs" options={['On the wearable', 'On the host PC']} choice="Host PC; the wearable receives only the result">
    Face-pose estimation needs far more compute than a small microcontroller has. Keeping it on the PC lets the wearable stay a small board with a module, an IMU and a motor driver.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Camera plus IMU" options={['Camera only', 'IMU only', 'Camera fused with the IMU']} choice="Fuse face pose from the camera with IMU data">
    Each sensor sees part of the posture. The IMU sits on the back and captures the curvature of the spine; the camera captures the position of the neck and shoulders, which the IMU can't see.
  </Decision>
</Decisions>

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection num="04" title="Verification">

- The PCB was fabricated, assembled and tested with the firmware and the vision pipeline running end to end.
- Haptic feedback reaches the wearer in under 200 ms.

</CaseSection>

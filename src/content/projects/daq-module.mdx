<CaseSection num="01" title="Problem">

One board had to read industrial sensors of three kinds and drive analog outputs back: voltage inputs, 4–20 mA current loops and RTD temperature sensors in, four voltage outputs out. It reports over Ethernet and RS-485 and runs from a 24 V supply.

The hard part is the mix. Twelve precision analog inputs sit on the same board as a switching regulator, an Ethernet PHY and a microcontroller.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection num="02" title="Architecture">

One AD4111 takes all twelve analog inputs: eight voltage inputs, usable as four differential pairs, and four current inputs. Two MAX31865 converters handle the RTDs. An AD3531 DAC feeds four op-amp buffers at a gain of 2 for a 0–10 V output range. The STM32H563 talks to all three converters over SPI, to a LAN8742A PHY over RMII and to the RS-485 transceiver over a UART.

<DaqBlock />

A 24 V input feeds an LT3514 triple buck. LDOs follow the buck where the load is analog: 13.5 V down to 12 V for the output buffers, 5.8 V down to 5 V, and 3.3 V down to 1.8 V for the DAC's logic. The 3.3 V output is also split through ferrite beads into digital, analog and PHY rails.

<CaseSection num="03" title="Key decisions">

<Decisions>
  <Decision title="A clean supply for the analog outputs" options={['Run the output buffers straight from the buck', 'Buck to 13.5 V, then an LDO to 12 V']} choice="13.5 V from the LT3514, regulated down to 12 V by an LDO">
    The buffers swing the outputs across 0–10 V, so their supply needs headroom above 10 V and as little switching ripple as possible. The LDO after the buck gives both.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Separate 3.3 V rails" options={['One 3.3 V rail for everything', 'Ferrite-filtered branches per function']} choice="Digital 3.3 V for the MCU, with filtered branches for the MCU's analog supply and the Ethernet PHY">
    Keeping digital switching noise off the MCU's analog supply and reference, and off the PHY, without adding another regulator.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Stable output into capacitive loads" options={['Op-amp drives the terminal directly', 'Isolation resistor with a feedback capacitor']} choice="10 Ω in series with the output, 47 pF from the op-amp output to its inverting input">
    A capacitive load on an op-amp output adds a pole inside the feedback loop and can make it ring. The resistor isolates the load; the capacitor closes a high-frequency loop around it, while the divider at the terminal still sets the DC gain.
  </Decision>
  <Decision title="Ethernet routing" options={['Route each line on its own', 'Coupled, length-matched pairs']} choice="TX and RX as 100 Ω differential pairs, length-matched">
    The PHY and the jack's magnetics expect a 100 Ω differential line. Matching keeps the two halves of each pair in phase.
  </Decision>
</Decisions>

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection num="04" title="Verification">

- The LT3514 stage was simulated in LTspice for start-up and steady-state ripple.
- The output stage was checked in a PSpice testbench against a 1 nF load.
- Layout rules cover clearance per area, differential pairs and power nets. The design-rule check finished with no violations.
- Schematic reviews logged each finding by severity.
